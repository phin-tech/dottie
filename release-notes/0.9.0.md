## dottie.js: big drawings play without holding up the page

- **Long, busy animations no longer freeze the page while they load.** Before showing an animation without a
  camera, `<dottie-player>` works out the view of every frame. For a big one that now happens in a worker, and the
  drawing appears when it's done, looking exactly as it would have. `setFile()` returns at once and `load` fires once
  the drawing is showing. Small drawings, stills and camera animations show straight away as before. Where a worker
  can't start, such as under a `worker-src` policy, the player works it out in place.
- If you host the module player yourself, serve `dottie-player.min.js` beside `dottie-player.js`: the worker loads
  it from there. The classic script, compiled drawings and the HTML export need nothing extra.
- **Faster everywhere.** On a 400-shape drawing with about 1,100 hopping connectors and traffic flows, getting
  ready to play is about nine times faster and each frame about fourteen times faster. Routing traffic, connector
  hops, notes, buttons' over and down looks, and labels with very long unbroken words all got quicker; a
  5,000-character word used to take minutes to wrap. Everything draws exactly as before.

## dottie.js fixes

- A compiled drawing whose text contains `DOTTIE_OPTIONS` or `DOTTIE_FONTS` no longer comes out broken.
- `setFile()` while a `src` is still loading: the file you gave stays, instead of being replaced when the load
  finishes.
- When a drawing fails to load, the one that was showing stops, and its controls go away.
- `currentTime` on a still (`at="5"`) reports the moment it shows, not 0.
